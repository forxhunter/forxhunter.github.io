I am a Ph.D. candidate in Biophysics and Quantitative Biology at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC), working in the Diwakar Shukla Group. My research is in AI for Biology (AI4Bio) and Protein Design, with a focus on automatic scientific discovery: building generative AI systems that propose scientific hypotheses and designs.

Lately I have been asking what these systems can know about conditions they have never observed, for example whether a virtual cell can predict a pathway it has never seen switch on, and how domain expertise can ship as a module that plugs into each new foundation model instead of a fork of an old one. The same question runs through my work on discovery systems: when two mechanistic explanations fit the same data equally well, which further measurement tells them apart?

My background is in AI for protein engineering, where I build models that predict and optimize physicochemical properties such as aggregation propensity, with applications including Rubisco engineering. Most recently I designed one hundred de novo binders for the five challenges of the 2026 Anthropic × Adaptyv competition. The hard part there was not generating candidates but choosing among them: on past competition data the usual interface confidence scores barely separate binders from non-binders, so every threshold in that pipeline is read off a control, and three gates were dropped for failing theirs.
